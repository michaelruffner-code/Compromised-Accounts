Part 1

How ATT Works

What ATT does with a reported email, what it shows you, and what each setting controls.

How it works

Cases, Verdicts, and Scores

Reported emails with the same sender and the same subject are grouped into one case. With ATT on, every case gets a verdict and a security score from 0 to 100, where 100 is the highest risk.

Verdict

Phishing

  • MeaningATT judged the email to be a phishing attack.
  • Rule actionAuto-quarantine, only when the score is at or above your threshold.
Verdict

Spam

  • MeaningATT judged the email to be unwanted but not phishing.
  • Rule actionAuto-remove spam. The setting describes this as removing spam from inboxes instead of just labeling it.
Verdict

Legitimate

  • MeaningATT judged the reported email to be safe.
  • Rule actionAuto-mark safe. The setting describes this as dismissing safe emails automatically.
How it works

The score measures security risk

The score reflects how dangerous the email is, not how much of a nuisance it is. A plain-looking credential harvesting email can score higher than an obvious mass marketing email.

Inside a Case

Sender and evidence details have been blurred. Click to enlarge.

  • Score, verdict, and status at the top of the case.
  • Key Evidence lists the signals behind the verdict and score, each tagged Critical, High, Medium, or Low.
  • ATT recommended actions lists next steps for this email: immediate actions, awareness actions for staff, and possible policy updates.
  • Remediate quarantines or deletes the email by sender or by entire domain. Restore undoes a quarantine or delete.
  • Advanced Threat Search opens a search pre-filled from the case to find copies nobody reported.
  • View Email opens the message and Export PDF exports the case.
  • Accounts Secured lists who received the email, and the audit log records every action on the case.

How it works

Settings Reference

All ATT settings are in Settings, Threat Manager. You can also open them from the gear icon next to Auto-remediation Enabled at the top right of Reported Threats. Every setting below is off until an admin turns it on.

SettingDefaultNeeds firstWhat it does
Auto-label reported threatsOffATT add-onGives every reported email a Phishing, Spam, or Legitimate verdict and a score. Labels only. Nothing is moved or removed.
Auto-RemediationOffAuto-label onThe main switch for the three rules below. No rule runs while this is off.
Phishing ruleOffAuto-Remediation onIf the verdict is Phishing and the score is at or above your threshold, the case is auto-quarantined. The threshold can be set from 70 to 99. The default is 80.
Spam ruleOffAuto-Remediation onIf the verdict is Spam, the reported email is removed automatically instead of just labeled.
Legitimate ruleOffAuto-Remediation onIf the verdict is Legitimate, the case is marked safe automatically.

Example account with all three rules on and the threshold set to 70.

How it works

Anything that doesn't match a rule goes to Needs Review

A case only moves automatically when its verdict matches a rule you turned on, and for Phishing, when its score is at or above your threshold. Everything else waits for a person.

How it works

The Spam and Legitimate rules act only on the reported email

They don't search other mailboxes, and they only act when ATT is at least 85 percent confident. That confidence level is fixed.

How it works

Reporters are told what happened

When a rule acts on a report, the reporter is sent a closed-the-loop email automatically, using your district's notification templates.

Set Up and Run ATT

A step-by-step rollout, then the habits that keep it accurate.

CyberNut

Roll Out ATT One Step at a Time

  1. Confirm ATT is onYou should see Powered by Automated Threat Terminator under the Reported Threats title, and Score and Verdict columns in the list.
  2. Check your closed-the-loop templatesOnce any rule is on, reporters get these emails automatically. Review the wording before that happens.
  3. Brief your Threat Manager adminsMake sure they know the audit log names: CyberNut for automated actions, a person's name for manual ones. Users with read-only access can't take case actions, so anyone working Needs Review needs full access.
  4. Turn on Auto-label reported threats onlyLeave Auto-Remediation off. For one to two weeks, compare ATT's verdicts with your own calls. Open Key Evidence on any verdict you disagree with.
  5. Turn on Auto-Remediation and the Phishing ruleKeep the threshold at the default of 80 to start.
  6. Add the Legitimate and Spam rules later, one at a timeGive each a few weeks. Turning on all three rules on day one automates decisions you haven't watched yet.

Recommended

Deciding on the Legitimate and Spam Rules

These two rules are your district's decision.

Legitimate rule

Good fit: a high volume of safe emails being reported

Safe reports such as stipend notices or vendor newsletters clear without anyone touching them, and the reporter is told it was safe. The tradeoff is that if ATT labels a threat as Legitimate, nobody reviews it. Turn it on only after your Legitimate verdicts have been consistently right.

Spam rule

Good fit: after about a month of accurate Spam verdicts

Spam is the least clear-cut verdict and the lowest risk, so reviewing it by hand costs little while you build confidence. Revisit it after about a month.

How it works · Recommended

The Phishing Threshold

The Phishing rule only quarantines cases scored at or above your threshold. You can set it anywhere from 70 to 99. The default is 80.

70Lowest setting 80Default 99Highest setting

A lower threshold quarantines more automatically and sends fewer cases to Needs Review. A higher threshold quarantines less automatically and sends more cases to you.

Recommended if

A legit email was auto-quarantined

Raise the threshold a few points, for example from 80 to 85, and keep watching. Adjust the threshold before turning the rule off.

Recommended if

Every automated quarantine was correct

If you're still quarantining obvious phishing in the 70s by hand after two to four weeks, lower the threshold toward 70.

Recommended

Re-check each quarter

Report volume changes over the school year. Review the audit log and confirm the threshold still fits.

Work What's Left in Needs Review

  1. Start with escalated casesThey're pinned to the top of Needs Review.
  2. Sort by ScoreWork from the highest score down.
  3. Check Reporter ActionsIf a reporter says they entered a password, see the password question below.

Troubleshooting

Common Questions

ATT quarantined an email someone says was legitimate. What do I do?
  • Open the case and read Key Evidence to see why ATT called it Phishing.
  • Check the audit log to confirm CyberNut took the action.
  • If the email is safe, use Restore on the case. Quarantined emails are deleted automatically after 30 days.
  • If it happens again, consider raising your threshold.
Why are cases still in Needs Review with auto-remediation on?

Rules only act on cases that match them. A Phishing case below your threshold, or a verdict whose rule is off, stays in Needs Review for a person.

ATT quarantined a phishing email, but someone already entered their password.

Quarantine removes the email but doesn't secure the account. Filter Reported Threats by credentials at risk to find these cases, then force a password reset from the case. You'll be asked to confirm. The user gets a temporary password, is signed out of every session, and must change it at next sign-in. You see the temporary password once so you can pass it on.

Someone reported an email we already marked Safe.

The case stays in the Safe tab. It isn't reopened or acted on automatically.

Should I use Mark Safe to undo an automated quarantine?

Mark Safe applies to the whole sender domain, not only that email. To bring back one email, use Restore instead.

What about phishing sent from one of our own accounts?

That is handled by Compromised Account Detection, which has its own settings under Settings, Compromised Accounts. See the Compromised Accounts guide.
