How It Works

What the feature does, what you see in the admin portal, and every setting that controls it.

How it works

What the Feature Does

An account is flagged only when all three of these happen, in order.

Check 1

Someone reports it

A staff member or student reports the email with the Report button.

Check 2

The sender is internal

The email came from an address on your own domain.

Check 3

It scores as Phishing

The AI gives it a Phishing verdict with a score at or above your threshold. Scores run from 0 to 100, and higher means riskier.

When an account is flagged

Flag only

  • Your team is alertedAn email goes to your alert recipients, or to the alerts address on the Report Button tab if none are set.
  • The account appears in Compromised AccountsYou can investigate the reported emails that led to the flag.
  • A person decides what happens nextThe account stays active until someone acts.
If auto-suspend is on

Flag and lock

  • The account is locked right awayThis works for both Google and Microsoft accounts.
  • IT admins are notifiedThe user has to contact IT to reset their password.
  • Reported emails are quarantinedCyberNut quarantines the reported emails automatically when auto-suspend is on.
Legitimate internal email won't flag an account. A mass email about stipends or prom tickets can be reported many times, but the account is only flagged if the AI gives it a Phishing verdict at or above your threshold.

How it works

API Permissions

CyberNut needs extra permissions in Google or Microsoft before you can turn on detection.

Google Workspace

Add two OAuth scopes

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/admin.directory.user https://www.googleapis.com/auth/admin.directory.user.security
  1. In the Google Admin Console, go to Security, Access and data control, API controls, then Manage Domain Wide Delegation.
  2. Click Edit on the CyberNut entry.
  3. Paste both scopes, then click Authorize and Confirm.

It can take up to 24 hours after you update the scopes before you can turn on detection.

Google setup steps on the Knowledge Hub

Microsoft 365

Add application permissions

  1. In the Microsoft Azure portal, add the Microsoft Graph and Office 365 Exchange Online application permissions listed in the Knowledge Hub article.
  2. Click Grant Admin Consent, next to Add a permission.
  3. Check that every permission shows Granted.

These permissions let CyberNut suspend an account directly through Microsoft's APIs.

Microsoft setup steps on the Knowledge Hub

How it works

What Suspending Does

Suspending is more than blocking email. The user and the attacker are signed out everywhere.

Microsoft 365

Locked through Microsoft's APIs

CyberNut disables the account, signs the user out of every session and device, and revokes their sign-in tokens. It changes the password to a random value that CyberNut does not store.

Google and Microsoft

Works on both

Auto-suspend works for Google and Microsoft accounts. The user has to contact IT to get back in.

Identity providers

Third party sign-in isn't supported

Suspend, auto-suspend, and reinstate work when Google or Microsoft is your primary identity provider. They don't currently work with a third party identity provider.

On-premises Active Directory or Exchange: a directory sync can reactivate a suspended account about every 30 minutes. Suspend the account in your own directory too.

Suspend Exceptions

Who to add

Leadership and shared mailboxes, where an automatic suspension would cause the most disruption. Excepted accounts are still flagged and still alert IT, so a person decides what happens next.

Lowering the threshold

A lower number catches more internal scams but flags more accounts. If auto-suspend is on, it also means more suspensions.

Recommended

Flag Only or Auto-Suspend?

This is a district decision. Answer these three questions.

Question 1

Would a suspension ripple through SSO?

Suspending signs the user out everywhere. If the account also opens your LMS, SIS, or badge access, a mid-day suspension has a wide impact.

If yes, lean flag only until you've tested it.

Question 2

Does policy require human review?

Some state or board policies expect a documented human decision before an account is disabled.

If yes, use flag only and log your response time.

Question 3

How fast can a person respond?

A team with round the clock coverage can act in minutes. A two person IT team that leaves at 4 p.m. can't.

If coverage is thin, auto-suspend is your safety net.

Larger district, staffed, complex SSO

Start with flag only

  • You get the alert and a person decides.
  • No risk of wrongly suspending a real user.
  • The account keeps sending until someone acts.
Smaller district, lean IT

Turn auto-suspend on

  • The attacker is cut off as soon as the account is flagged.
  • Covers 2 a.m. on a Saturday.
  • Only accounts at or above your threshold are suspended.
Either way, revisit your choice after your first real incident.

Review a Flagged Account

  1. Open the alert or the Compromised Accounts pageFind the flagged account.
  2. Check the scoreTreat 80 or higher with clear evidence as act now.
  3. Read the Key EvidenceLook for a credential harvesting link, a fake login page, or a request that doesn't fit the sender's role.
  4. If it's a real compromiseSuspend the account if auto-suspend didn't already, then work through the Recommended Actions from top to bottom.
  5. If it's a real but unusual internal emailLeave the account active, or reinstate it if it was suspended. Be careful with Mark Safe. It trusts the whole sender domain, which here is your own.

Stop an Active Outbreak

  1. Suspend the accountThis signs the attacker out of every session, so they can't send another email.
  2. Search for every copyOpen the case, then Advanced Threat Search. The subject line fills in for you. Set the time frame to cover the whole send window.
  3. Delete the caseRemove the email from every affected inbox before more people click.
  4. Notify staffUse the staff awareness Recommended Actions as your script. Anyone who clicked should reset their password now.
  5. Reinstate only when it's cleanFollow the reinstate steps below once the mailbox is clean.

Reinstate a Suspended Account

  1. Open the accountGo to Compromised Accounts and open the dropdown on the account entry.
  2. Click Reinstate AccountChoose Case Resolved or False Positive. Both work the same for the user and for you. False Positive helps CyberNut improve detection.
  3. Copy the new passwordCyberNut generates a new password and shows it to you. The old password no longer works.
  4. Give the password to the userThe user contacts IT to get it, and is prompted to change it at their next sign in.

Before you reinstate

Check the Key Evidence and confirm the bad emails are deleted. Look for forwarding rules or anything else the attacker left behind.

Recommended

Hand off the password safely

Give it in person or by phone. Never by email. If the mailbox was compromised, an emailed password goes straight to the attacker.

Reinstatement considerations
  • Reinstating doesn't rerun workflows in your other systems. Set up MFA and other downstream access again through your normal process.
  • Google Workspace: if a reinstatement doesn't carry over to Google, reinstate the account in the Google Admin Console.
  • On-premises Active Directory or Exchange: a directory sync can reactivate a suspended account about every 30 minutes. Suspend the account in your own directory too.

Report, never forward

A forwarded email comes from the person who forwarded it, so the internal sender check fails and the account is never flagged.

Check Compromised Accounts daily for your first month, then weekly, plus every time an alert comes in.

Common Questions

Do we need the ATT add-on?
No. Compromised Account Detection is part of the standard license at no extra cost. Flagged accounts show the score, Key Evidence, and Recommended Actions without ATT.
Why can't we turn on detection?
The API permissions are usually missing or still processing. For Google, confirm both OAuth scopes are on the CyberNut entry in Domain Wide Delegation. It can take up to 24 hours after you add them. For Microsoft, confirm every permission in the Azure portal shows Granted.
Will a reported internal email always flag the account?
No. The email has to be reported, sent from your own domain, and get a Phishing verdict at or above your threshold.
Does auto-suspend work for Google and Microsoft?
Yes. It works for both Google and Microsoft accounts. It does not currently work if your district signs in through a third party identity provider.
What happens to accounts on the exception list?
They are never auto-suspended, even on a BEC verdict. They are still flagged, and IT admins are still notified.
Where do alerts go, and how many people can get them?
Alerts go to the addresses you enter, up to 10. If you leave the list empty, they go to the alerts address on the Report Button tab.
Does CyberNut keep the user's password?
No. When a Microsoft 365 account is suspended, the password is changed to a random value that CyberNut does not store. When you reinstate, a new password is generated and shown to you.
What's the difference between Case Resolved and False Positive?
Nothing changes for the user or for you. Choosing False Positive tells CyberNut the detection was wrong, so it can improve.
